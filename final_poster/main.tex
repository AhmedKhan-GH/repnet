  \begin{block}{Introduction}

    Preeclampsia is a time-sensitive hypertensive disorder of pregnancy that
    can escalate to eclamptic seizures within days if undiagnosed. It is
    currently identified clinically only after $\geq$20 weeks of gestation
    via elevated blood pressure and end-organ markers such as proteinuria.
    Because the only definitive treatment is delivery, \textbf{early
    prediction is critical} --- it opens a window for monitoring, lifestyle
    change, and pharmaceutical intervention before the disease progresses.

    \heading{Goal}

    Predict preeclampsia risk weeks to months before clinical diagnosis
    using \textbf{quantitative features extracted from 12-lead ECGs} paired
    with interpretable tree-based and attention-based models.

  \end{block}

  \begin{block}{Related Work}

    \begin{itemize}
      \item \textbf{ECG + deep learning:} A modified 1D-ResNet on raw
        12-lead signals can flag preeclampsia 30--90 days before diagnosis,
        but was validated primarily on an African-American cohort, leaving
        demographic generalization open.
      \item \textbf{Tree-based EHR models:} LightGBM and XGBoost on blood,
        urine, and vitals highlight age and lab biomarkers as dominant
        features --- strong baselines, but not wearable-friendly.
      \item \textbf{Lead reduction:} Prior work shows leads I, II, V2, V4
        can retain most diagnostic morphology, motivating a path toward
        single-lead smart-wearable deployment.
    \end{itemize}

  \end{block}

  \begin{alertblock}{Contribution}

    We build an \textbf{explainable} preeclampsia risk pipeline that pairs
    \emph{engineered} ECG features (dispersions, Tp-e, QRS-T angle,
    ST deviations) with \emph{learned} representations, so each prediction
    can be traced back to a cardiologically meaningful quantity.

    \begin{itemize}
      \item Feature extraction built on top of the NeuroKit2 library,
        cross-validated against MUSE values from UC Davis Health.
      \item Decision-tree baselines (RF, LightGBM, XGBoost) with SMOTE
        balancing and $\chi^2$/PCA feature-importance analysis.
      \item Attention-based extension mapping transformer attention back
        onto the engineered features for interpretability.
    \end{itemize}

  \end{alertblock}

\end{column}

  \begin{block}{Engineered ECG Features}

    From delineated P, QRS, and T events across 12 leads we compute a
    compact feature vector emphasizing \textbf{repolarization heterogeneity}
    and \textbf{ST-segment morphology}, both implicated in hypertensive
    cardiac stress.

    \begin{table}
      \centering
      \small
      \begin{tabular}{l l}
        \toprule
        \textbf{Feature} & \textbf{Definition} \\
        \midrule
        P-wave dispersion     & $P_{\max} - P_{\min}$ across 12 leads \\
        QT dispersion         & $QT_{\max} - QT_{\min}$ across 12 leads \\
        Tp-e interval         & mean($T_{\text{offset}} - T_{\text{peak}}$) \\
        Tp-e / QTc            & rate-corrected Tp-e ratio \\
        ST elevation / depr.  & $>$ 50\% threshold indicators \\
        Amplitude diffs       & avg / med / max / min (ST $-$ PR) \\
        QRS axis              & isoelectric-lead \& Novosel arctan \\
        T axis                & analogous to QRS axis \\
        Frontal QRS-T angle   & $|\text{QRS axis} - \text{T axis}|$ \\
        \bottomrule
      \end{tabular}
      \caption{Core engineered ECG features.}
    \end{table}

  \end{block}

  \begin{block}{Validation Strategy}

    \begin{itemize}
      \item \textbf{Nightingale ECG dataset} for pipeline sanity-checking
        (per-lead CSV ingestion path).
      \item \textbf{UCDH Echo dataset} (balanced, MUSE-annotated) for
        comparing NeuroKit-derived features against clinical MUSE values.
      \item Three ECGs dropped for zero-filled leads or NaNs after
        delineation; all drops logged for reproducibility.
    \end{itemize}

  \end{block}

  \begin{block}{Evaluation Metrics}

    Preeclampsia prediction is framed as a \textbf{binary classification}
    problem. We report a balanced metric suite to expose both class-imbalance
    behavior and clinical usefulness:

    $$
      \text{AUC}, \;\; \text{Accuracy}, \;\; \text{Precision}, \;\;
      \text{Recall}, \;\; F_1
    $$

    Since the positive class is rare, we prioritize \textbf{AUC and recall}
    and treat accuracy alone as misleading. All experiments run with
    stratified $k$-fold cross-validation.

  \end{block}

    \textbf{Open issue:} QRS- and T-axis calculation. Two implementations
    are currently under evaluation ---
    (1) the \emph{isoelectric-lead method} ($\pm 90^{\circ}$ from the
    perpendicular pair) and
    (2) the \emph{Novosel formula}
    $\text{QRS axis} = \pm \arctan\!\left(\tfrac{2 \cdot aVF - III}{I}\right)$.
    Clinically, small degree differences are rarely actionable, so the
    pipeline will lean on the simpler isoelectric estimate unless axis is
    flagged as high-importance.

  \end{block}

  \begin{block}{Future Work}

    \begin{itemize}
      \item Feature-importance analysis ($\chi^2$, PCA) to prune the
        engineered feature set.
      \item Compare RF vs.\ LightGBM vs.\ XGBoost on the balanced UCDH
        Echo dataset, with SMOTE on training folds only.
      \item Extend to a \textbf{single-lead} model compatible with smart
        wearables (Apple Watch-style lead-I signals).
      \item Add transformer attention maps projected back onto the
        engineered features for clinician-facing explanations.
    \end{itemize}

  \end{block}
